## 3. What went well

**Wolf, in his own words:** *"We delivered some sort of game already. Maybe not the greatest game yet
but hope that it's easy to build on top of this. Groundwork seem to now benefit. For example creating
and adding new animations was butter smooth operation end to end."*

- **The animation rail.** 12.5 set up name-bound clips driven by wire state. 12.8's cut clip went on the same rail in **one art round** ($21.59, at a seat Wolf could watch). The dwarf model took 13 blind rounds and $891. The M2-24 art hard stop never had to fire.
- **The sim's extension pattern.** Each new piece of state follows the same steps: a purpose-named stream, a component, the wire, the save, an exhaustive bridge. Identity, profession, item kind, ore and goals all went in this way. 12.3, 12.6 and 12.1 were the three cheapest stories.
- **12.1's refusal shape** (typed `Delta.refusals`) was reused unchanged by 12.6 (`SetProfession`) and 12.7 (`Designate`). NFR11 held across every new filter.
- **Wire instruments written at story creation, RED on main.** Each one later caught a real defect, and they were hardened along the way: exit 2 means "proves nothing". A raw-socket tap found Nagle clumping (#200): stock clumped on 4 of 8 connections, NODELAY on 0 of 12.
- **Seed and delay sweeps turned livelocks into deterministic cases.**
  - 12.10b's delay sweep went from 16/24 to 24/24.
  - Its 1,200-run probe took frozen runs from 4 to 0 (one-wide) and from 13 to 0 (two-wide).
  - 12.9 used a 32-run cage sweep.
- **The stopping rule** capped review spend and never cut off a real HIGH. Run 3 was authorised only after a new HIGH (12.9, 12.10b, 12.12), and no round 4 ever ran.
- **Task 0 rulings with measured prototypes** became the norm from 12.4 on: A vs B prototypes, 12.11's pure-gravity sweep at 17/24, and 12.12's four target sets.

## 4. What was hard — Wolf's ranking, most costly first

Wolf ranked three items: the movement layer, then performance, then the gate. **Seat findings are explicitly NOT on the list.** In 10 of 13 stories the seat found what the tests did not: stones thrown 5 cells, the *"suction"* pick-up, *"felt heavy"*. Wolf: *"something what is probably difficult to test without the seat and I am not against of testing via the seat. That's what I need to do more to get the feel of the game right."* The seat is the feel instrument, and a seat finding is the loop working (§9, team agreement).

### 4.1 The movement layer is ad-hoc — not only stuck dwarves

Stuck, caged or looping dwarves appeared in **8 of 13 stories** (12.1, 12.3, 12.4, 12.9, 12.10, 12.10b, 12.11, 12.12). **Eight open issues** are this family: #159 #186 #187 #189 #203 #204 #206 #207. Wolf widened the item: *"a bit wider than just stuck dwarves and I think it caused also perf issues. Pathfinding also might be something what we want to re-check … are we following industry standard best practices or just doing ad-hoc fixing?"*

**Finding: ad-hoc.** From `crates/sim-core/src/lib.rs` at `ac0a2f8`:
- **No reachability is kept between ticks.** `lib.rs:569`: *"terrain can change between ticks, so nothing is cached across them."* Every "can he get there?" question is a fresh A* or flood, each bounded by `MAX_ASTAR_NODES = 50_000`. That covers claims, the wall-in checks, the pile-ring rule and the caging check. The bound itself is what produces #159's starvation.
- **The A\* is a plain implementation.** Costs and came-from are `BTreeMap`s, and the open set is a `BinaryHeap`. The heuristic (`lib.rs:1124`) loops over **every goal cell at every expansion**, so a haul to a 518-cell pile pays 518 distance checks per node. That plausibly explains why "a big pile costs ~3.5 ms/tick on main" (12.12 dev).
- **Four local guards answer one cell each and conflict:**
  - `walls_in_a_dwarf` (12.9);
  - the wall-in check on cut terrain (12.10b);
  - `closes_last_level_exit` and `caging_stone` (12.11);
  - the pile-ring entrance rule (12.12).

  None of them asks whether every dwarf can still reach work. #207 is a guard refusing a drop because it would wall in a hauler, and that refusal is the stall itself. Each fix closed one cage shape, and the next shape appeared at the seat.

**Genre practice** (leads from a quick search, to be verified by M3-1):
- **Dwarf Fortress** uses whole-map A* ([DF wiki: Path](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Path)). From memory, not verified: DFHack exposes a per-tile walkable-group id, which would mean a connectivity map sits beside the A*.
- **Factorio** replaced a straight-line heuristic that broke on long routes around obstacles ([FFF #317](https://cf-www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-317)). From memory: the replacement is a coarse search over chunk components.
- **Going Medieval**, a 3D voxel colony sim, rebuilt pathfinding that was tangled with the rest of the game ([MMT #18](https://foxyvoxel.io/2022/03/14/mmt18/)). Dropped items **slow** travel there, to 70%, rather than block it.

**Wolf ruled the strict rules stay:** *"I still want to keep stricter rules like one-dwarf per tile and blocking items when it is natural .. ofc one option is to solve it with animations."* That is the genre's standard trick: the sim allows a slow, costed move, and the gui sells it.
- **Squeeze past:** a timed swap in a one-wide way. At most one dwarf *stands* on a tile.
- **Clamber over:** a costed step over a blocking stone.

Both stay strict in spirit and remove most cage shapes. They are measured in M3-1, then ruled.

### 4.2 Performance invisible to tests

No test asserts cost, and every regression was found by someone choosing to measure:
- **12.12:** agent A's entrance rule cost **17×** the tick (0.17 → 2.88 ms mean) with every test green. In run 2, the one union box failed on spread piles (19.84 vs 4.97 ms; fast4x 17 vs 95 t/s), because the patch pass had measured only compact layouts.
- **12.9:** spikes of 9–17.6 ms, traced to a single `astar_with_budget` call against the 5 ms fast4x period (#179).
- **12.10b:** Nagle clumping (#200) pulsed the gui while the tui looked fine.

Wolf: the perf test is *"not too difficult to run … but we should have scripted scenarios for it so no need to manually play to make it more robust and reproducible"*. He can watch gui FPS during the same scripted run. That gives one scenario file and two consumers: a headless `simd` tick benchmark, and a seat run with `--perf-log`. → M3-2.

### 4.3 The gate — *"it takes ages to run so progress is slow"*

- The full gate grew from 2,652 s (12.1) to **3,700–5,000 s**, green only at `RUST_TEST_THREADS=1`.
- 12.12's last block: **pixel guards 3,011 s of 3,779 (80%)**, `cargo test` 725 s.
- What the guards are: 17 tests, about 27 daemon-plus-gui launches, each rendering 160 frames at 1280×720 through lavapipe, in series. **8 of the 17 check logs or the wire, not pixels**, and still pay for full frames.
- 12.12 ran the full gate about four times (dev plus three patch passes).

Skipping the pixel guards on sim-only diffs was rejected: *"we have a lot of gui changes coming so skipping with pure simd stories might not be helping that much"*. Wolf ruled two things instead:
- **optimize the guards** (→ M3-3, which folds in #145);
- **one full gate per story, at PR** (→ M3-4).

## 7. Key insights

1. **The groundwork pays.** The animation rail, the extension pattern and the refusal shape each made the next story cheap. Build M4 on them.
2. **A movement layer built from local guards can't be finished one cage at a time.** Reachability that is recomputed per question costs both correctness and ticks. Fix the layer (M3-1), not the next shape.
3. **The seat is the feel instrument, and that is by design.** Scripted scenarios (M3-2) make seat sittings repeatable, so they don't need replacing.
4. **Cost needs its own instrument.** Tests are blind to it by construction, and in 12.12 "measure only the compact layout" let the same class through twice.
5. **The gate's cost lives in one tier.** 80% is pixel guards, and half of those don't check pixels.
