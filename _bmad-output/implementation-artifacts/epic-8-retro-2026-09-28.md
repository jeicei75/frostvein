## 3. What went well

**Wolf, in his own words:** *"We achieved a lot. And I really mean a lot what comes to look and feel,
wow, process, gui, gd pipelines, perf testing etc. … this would have already taken one full small game
team … and we did it even with rust instead of C++, Unreal, Unity. Frostvein performs pretty decently
and looks good."*

- **The spine held.** Twenty stories of look work, and not one put game logic in a client. The
  dependency-edge probe stayed green throughout, and determinism was extended to trees and lights.
- **The seat as final judge worked.** Every closure was Wolf's eye, and several defects that no
  instrument caught were caught there:
  - the floating dwarf (10.5);
  - the F1/F2 key collision (11.2);
  - the inert haze toggle (11.2);
  - the k=16 dig halts (10.6).
- **Engineering wins:**
  - The dig stall went from ~2.5 s to 55 ms (10.6).
  - Tree cost dropped 704→265 trees (9.4), and the terrain from 927k to ~100k triangles (10.9).
  - A deterministic headless bench was built (10.1; 0 of 2,073,600 pixels differ between runs).
  - A committed tree generator reproduces the assets byte-identically (10.2).
  - Frames per second (fps) at working zoom stayed above 100 on the vehicle through most of the pass.
- **Commit-per-task cadence** saved every delegated run that was killed or ran out of quota.
- **The "move forward" ruling** (2026-09-26, *"need to soon move forward or we tweak this story
  forever"*) closed 11.3 and 8.3 in two days.

## 4. What was hard — Wolf's ranking, most costly first

### 4.1 The dwarf — *"I almost lost my faith"*

It took 13 delegated BlenderMCP modelling rounds (10.5 / 10.5b) and $891 without sign-off, and none of it
was on a story ledger. Three spec metrics could be gamed (Bevel chamfer strips, a spacer in the bounding
volume hierarchy), and the seat gamed them, so every round "passed" something.

The recorded diagnosis: **a modelling agent that cannot see what it selects is the wrong tool for
hand-authored art.** Nothing stopped the loop, and nothing made its spend visible.
→ Action item M2-24.

### 4.2 The gate — *"when I want to test live it hinders me"*

The full gate grew from **67 s to 1,700–1,930 s**, most of it pixel guards. It has to run at
`RUST_TEST_THREADS=2` or it runs out of memory, and it can bring WSL to its knees and disturb other
devpods; once it killed WSL outright. The fast tier on commit takes 50–95 s.

Wolf's vehicle loop, as he runs it:
1. Build simd and tui.
2. Cross-build gui.exe for Windows.
3. Start simd in WSL.
4. Copy the binary to Windows.
5. Pull the branch on Windows.
6. Launch.

This is "relatively easy and fast as long as the branch is pushed". Two frictions remain:
- **Push is slow.** The 90 s push gate re-runs the fast tier on commits the pre-commit hook already
  gated.
- **The launcher breaks copied builds.** `launch-gui.ps1` fetches the branch tip, so any commit that
  lands after the copy breaks the setup.

→ Action items M2-28 to M2-31.

are damn difficult to decide from screenshots"*. For SIM work, deterministic scenario tests are.

### 4.6 Scope growth — *not* the problem it looked like

Charlie raised the growth: 28 stories against a cap of 10–14, Epic 10 growing from 5 to 11, look
loops inside stories (10.8's candidate tables A/B/C/F; 11.3's moon lux → haze gain → moon disc → sun
disc). Alice countered that every addition was ruled by Wolf and grounded in a real defect (the

**Wolf's ruling:** *"I am not afraid of scope growing as long as it's growing for supporting original
scope and also benefits the game progress."*

**Clarified:** *"creating new stories are ok … that we are doing in main forge all the time … it's
quite fast and we don't need to change that. I mean creating completely new Epics all the time for all
new features is not ok if we already have somewhat related Epic."*

What was frustrating was *"to go through full epic process just to see some new features on live"*.
By this rule, Epic 10's growth was fine. Opening Epic 11 when Epic 10 was already "the look" is the
questionable part. → Action item M2-25.

## 5. Recurring review-finding classes across Epics 8–11

1. **Inert seams, each time one level further out.**
   - From the product into the instruments: `--cursor` parsed then dropped (8.1); the process exits
     0 on failure (8.2); `--drag` passes with 0 marks.
   - Into the tests: a hit face that stayed 149/149 green when inverted.
   - Into the sim's own acceptance.
2. **The `MinimalPlugins` harness is blind to the real app.**
   - `GlobalTransform` stays frozen at the origin.
   - `just_pressed` is sticky.
   - Key collisions don't show.
   - `remove_with_requires` crashing `sync_world` doesn't show.
3. **Self-referential oracles.**
   - Pick tests built the cursor from the same projection as the pick.
   - `tree_meshes` sits on both sides of its own check.
   - The watcher test asserted its own input.
   - 10.9's "flat" control was the function's own ice branch.
4. **Mutation-table integrity.**
   - "KILLED" names the test, not the assertion.
   - Rows drifted or stranded (8 of 518 after 10.8).
   - A reviewer executed the payloads and wrote into 5 tracked files (10.10).
   - A mutant binary outlived the source restore.
5. **Frame-count waits calibrated to one GPU.** Three broke on the 4080. The rule now: waits go in
   wall clock or ticks.
6. **Record drift.**
   - Stale File Lists and story headers (10.8 and 11.1b still said `in-progress`; fixed here).
   - Boxes left unticked after merge.
   - Fixed issues left open (#46, #75, #77, #98, #105, #107).
   - One commit moved two colours, and the docs were updated for only one.

## 6. Key insights

1. **Live gate early for look work; scenario tests for sim work.** The seat is the instrument for what
   screenshots cannot decide. M3 is sim work, which is where this project's strongest instrument (a
   deterministic scenario on a fixed seed) lives.
2. **Stories are cheap; epics are not.** New work joins the nearest related epic as a story.
3. **Every delegated loop needs a hard stop and a ledger row**, or it burns silently.
4. **Reproduce before you plan.** Records invent scope. Code and a red test do not.
5. **The gate must be a good neighbour.** It shares one machine with other devpods, and a gate that
   starves them costs more than it saves.
