# Round 4 — model the dwarf live, with Wolf steering

**This is not round 3 with more words. It is a different kind of session.** Rounds 1–3 were
unattended: a long brief went out, a finished asset came back, and the look was judged only at the
end. Twice the session delivered exactly what was asked and the result was still wrong. So this
round is **steered**: Wolf watches, you stop and ask, and the brief carries invariants instead of a
feature list.

Read the whole thing before the first tool call. The **Loop** section is the part that changes how
you work; the **Invariants** section is the part that is not negotiable; everything about the look
is Wolf's, live.

## What is being replaced, and what is NOT

**Start the model from scratch.** The shipped dwarf's geometry and the 948-line procedural
generator `dwarf_miner.py` are being retired as the authoring path — they are why nothing was ever
watchable and why the shape could only be changed by editing hardcoded voxel tables.

**Do not start the CONTRACT from scratch.** The shipped asset passes `check_asset.py` at exit 0 and
carries work that was never the problem: grid alignment, `min Y = 0`, X/Z centring, one mesh / one
material / one palette image, and a **188-byte** palette atlas. All of that stays. What you are
replacing is the dwarf, not the pipeline.

**The `.blend` is now the SOURCE OF TRUTH.** This is the real change in this round. Previously the
generator was the source and the `.blend` a by-product; from now on the `.blend` is the asset and a
script exports it. Two consequences, both stated plainly because one of them is a loss:

- A hand-modelled mesh cannot be regenerated byte-identically from a script, so the old finishing
  condition — *"the committed generator regenerates the committed GLB exactly"* — **retires with
  this round.** Do not try to satisfy it; do not write a script that dumps voxel tables to fake it.
- What replaces it: the `.blend` is committed as the source, the GLB is **exported from it by a
  script rather than by hand**, and `check_asset.py` plus the revision token police the result. The
  export must be repeatable from the committed `.blend` by one command.

## Naming — one rule, and it is a deliverable

**Source and export share the stem.** Today one asset wears three names (`dwarf.blend`,
`dwarf_miner.py`, `SM_VoxelDwarf_Miner01.glb`), which is the confusion itself.

| what | path |
|---|---|
| the source, hand-modelled | `src-assets/blender/SM_VoxelDwarf_Miner01.blend` |
| the exporter | `src-assets/blender/export_dwarf.py` |
| the export (scratch, gitignored) | `src-assets/export/SM_VoxelDwarf_Miner01.glb` |
| the runtime slot — **you never write here** | `assets/gltf/SM_VoxelDwarf_Miner01.glb` |

- **Rename `dwarf.blend` to `SM_VoxelDwarf_Miner01.blend`.** `dwarf.blend` says nothing about which
  GLB it produces; the new name says it exactly.
- **`01` is the ROLE, not a version** — miner as against a future smith. Never bump it to `Miner02`
  to mean "second attempt".
- **The revision lives in the datablock names INSIDE the `.blend`, not in any filename.** Name the
  object and mesh datablocks `SM_VoxelDwarf_Miner01_r4`, the material `M_VoxelDwarf_r4`, the image
  `T_VoxelDwarf_Palette_r4`. The exporter carries those into the GLB, so there is exactly one place
  to bump a revision and a stale binary announces itself. (Why it matters: this project has shipped
  two different meshes both called `SM_VoxelPine_Tree02` and spent a story working out which
  binary was which.)
- Leave the pines' names alone. They have the same inconsistency and it is not this round's job.

## The Loop — how this session runs

1. **Author inside WOLF'S RUNNING BLENDER, through the MCP addon.** Not in a
   `blender --background` subprocess. Round 3 ran headless, so Wolf sat in front of an open Blender
   and watched nothing happen for an entire session. If a step is easier as a spawned background
   process, **it is still not allowed for authoring** — only the final export may run headless.
2. **Work in parts, one step at a time, in this order:** proportions blockout (a featureless
   silhouette) → head → beard → hair → torso → arms → legs → boots → belt → pack → tools.
3. **After EVERY step, three things, in this order:**
   - **save the `.blend`** — this is the resume point, and it is not optional. If the session dies
     (four of six delegated runs in this project have been killed by the harness), everything since
     the last save is gone.
   - **write a render** to `src-assets/renders/progress/NN-<part>.png`, numbered in order. Each one
     must show **the whole figure, not just the new part** — plus a small-scale view of it (~60 px
     tall) beside the full-size one. Judging only the newest part is how a session optimises every
     piece and still produces a character nobody likes.
   - **STOP AND ASK.** Say what you did, name the file you wrote, and wait.
4. **When you ask, you stop.** Do not carry on to be helpful. Do not batch three parts and then
   ask. A session that asks and then keeps working has spent Wolf's attention and given him
   unreviewed work anyway, which is worse than not asking.
5. **If Wolf corrects you, change the model, re-render, re-save, and show it again** before moving
   on. A correction is not an instruction for the next part; it is work on this one.
6. **Never touch git.** No `git add`, `commit`, `checkout`, `stash`, `branch` — nothing. Write files
   at the paths in this brief and leave them there; the operator commits. This is deliberate: an
   authoring seat with git access is one bad call away from rewriting history.
7. **Never write outside `src-assets/`.** In particular `assets/gltf/` is the running game's
   directory and is promoted by the operator, never by you.

## Invariants — not negotiable, and not Wolf's to relitigate mid-session

- **`min Y = 0`**, the figure standing on the origin plane; **centred in X and Z**.
- **Voxel-aligned geometry.** Every vertex on the authored voxel lattice. Model in voxels, not in
  free-form mesh: box-shaped parts, axis-aligned faces, no bevels, no non-axis normals.
- **DO NOT RAISE THE RESOLUTION.** Measured off the reference at t=10 s: the `dwarf.mp4` dwarf is
  roughly **60–65 voxels tall** and its belt buckle is about **5 voxels** across. The shipped asset
  is **96 voxels** — already finer than the target, and still reading flatter. Work at or below the
  reference's resolution. If you want a number, 64 voxels tall is a good default.
- **NO SURFACE GROOVING.** Round 3's predecessor cut a 1-voxel channel every 3 voxels across the
  beard and tunic to add "detail"; at 96 voxels that is corduroy, and it is the "vertical voxel
  bands" Wolf rejected. Detail belongs in the **silhouette** (locks that notch the outline, a
  stepped hem, stepped cuffs) and in **value steps** — the reference's tunic carries at least three
  greens where ours carries one.
- **One mesh, one material, one palette image.** Colour comes from palette cells in a small atlas,
  not from multiple materials. A cell's meaning is fixed by its coordinate, so a recolour is a table
  of hexes — that is what makes hundreds of variants nearly free, and the atlas is 188 bytes.
- **Unwelded quads, and no quad may cross a joint.** Measured on the shipped asset: of 28,796
  vertices, **zero** share position+normal+UV, so there is nothing to weld that would not also
  average normals across hard voxel edges and destroy the voxel read. Keep parts separable at joint
  planes — a quad spanning shoulder to wrist cannot bend, it can only shear.
- **Beard and hair must lift off.** Each is one part on one declared socket on the head, shaped so
  that removing it leaves a complete head underneath: no skin borrowed from the beard's volume, no
  hairline that only works with this hair. They are the first variant axes Wolf named, and the beard
  is the largest single read on the character.
- **Tools on hand sockets** — `hand.L` the lantern, `hand.R` the pickaxe — never fused into the
  torso. They become separate assets later.
- **A skeleton with RIGID weights**, if you rig at all this round: every vertex 1.0 to one joint, no
  blending. Soft skinning smears cubes. Joint names, fixed now so one clip can drive every variant:
  `root, hips, spine, chest, neck, head, shoulder.L/R, elbow.L/R, hand.L/R, hip.L/R, knee.L/R,
  foot.L/R`, plus `beard`. **Rig LAST, after Wolf has accepted the shape** — and expect
  `check_asset.py` to reject a rigged GLB, because the v1 contract allows one node and a skin adds
  joint nodes. That is the contract not having caught up. Report the failure text verbatim; do not
  loosen the checker and do not strip the skin to make it pass.

## The reference

`src-assets/references/dwarf.mp4` is the authority — **the video, not the contact sheet.** Sample it
with `ffmpeg -i dwarf.mp4 -vf fps=1` and hold the t=1/4/7/10 s frames beside your renders. What to
take from it: chunky voxels, big value steps inside one material, detail in the silhouette, rigid
posed limbs, a lantern that reads as the brightest thing on the character.

What NOT to take from it: the lighting. Soft shadows and the warm pool on the floor are the
renderer's, not the asset's, and they are explicitly out of scope — Wolf: *"no need to worry about
lighting etc .. just model first."* An emissive flame cell is allowed; nothing else about light is
yours this round.

**One thing you cannot read from that file, so do not guess:** whether each material carries a fine
surface texture. It is 3.4 MB for 10 s at 1280x720 and at 8x the mottling is indistinguishable from
compression. If you want texture, propose it with a render and let Wolf rule.

## Deliverables — the full manifest, stated here rather than inherited

Round 3 shipped no `.blend` because the requirement lived two documents away, in an earlier round's
table, reached by "everything else holds unchanged". That is on the brief, not only on the session.
So, in full — every path, every time:

| # | what | path |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the source, hand-modelled, **saved after every step** | `src-assets/blender/SM_VoxelDwarf_Miner01.blend` |
| 2 | the exporter, repeatable in one command | `src-assets/blender/export_dwarf.py` |
| 3 | the exported GLB (gitignored scratch) | `src-assets/export/SM_VoxelDwarf_Miner01.glb` |
| 4 | progress renders, one per accepted step | `src-assets/renders/progress/NN-<part>.png` |
| 5 | the five final views | `src-assets/renders/` (as `render_dwarf.py` writes them) |
| 6 | the zoom strip: 10 / 30 / 100 px and full height | `src-assets/renders/zoom-strip.png` |
| 7 | your report | `src-assets/prompts/dwarf-miner-round-4-report.md` |
| 8 | the checker's output, verbatim, pass or fail | in the report |
| 9 | your cost | in the report — `session_tokens.py` in print mode against your own transcript, pasted verbatim, the model read from your session banner, row labelled **`dev-art`** |

**The zoom strip is a judgement tool, not decoration.** Free zoom is the plan and the game is also
the marketing renderer, so the same asset must hold from a few pixels to full screen. A detail that
turns to speckle at 10 px is a **defect**: sub-pixel detail does not politely vanish, it aliases,
and aliasing shimmers as the camera moves.

## How this round will be judged

Not by a feature list. By Wolf's eye, live, against the video — plus these, which are mechanical and
either true or not:

- no 1-voxel channel repeating at a fixed step anywhere on the asset
- no quad crossing a joint
- the beard lifts off and leaves a complete head
- nothing turns to speckle at 10 px on the zoom strip
- every internal datablock name carries `r4`
- a `.blend` exists at the path above and the GLB exports from it by one command
- nothing written outside `src-assets/`, and no git command run at all
